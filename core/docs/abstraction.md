# Concretization and abstraction

An abstraction expresses a way of understanding a relationship. A concretization is a relationship understood through that abstraction. These are relative roles in a judgment, not permanent categories of things.

```abstract
engine.car::part.whole
```

This judgment expresses an understanding of the engine–car relationship through the part–whole relationship. Its correspondence places `engine` in the `part` role and `car` in the `whole` role. The claim concerns their relationship as a whole, not two independent classifications of its endpoints.

The same concrete expression may participate in another judgment:

```abstract
engine.car::power_unit.mechanism
```

The same expression can be interpreted through another abstraction. Its matching words coincide across these contexts by default. An author can detach a contextual term when a different concept is intended, or explicitly identify selected terms. Sharing concept identity does not make the two abstraction judgments equivalent.

Similarity, analogy and abstraction should not be silently substituted for one another. Sharing features can motivate a comparison. An analogy can suggest a correspondence. A concretization judgment makes the further commitment that one relationship is understood as a realization of the other relationship under that correspondence. Neither resemblance nor a proposed correspondence automatically establishes this commitment.

Abstract records the commitment explicitly. It does not determine from word meanings whether the chosen abstraction is justified. Whether the abstraction is justified, and why its correspondence is appropriate, are questions for the interpretation’s author. A well-formed judgment can still express an interpretation that someone disputes or later revises.
